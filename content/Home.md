## **On the manifesto and anti-manifesto**

These are powerful when done right and tedious when done wrong. The trap most companies fall into: manifestos become abstract, philosophical posturing that nobody reads. The best ones are specific, opinionated, and uncomfortable.

### **What works for a manifesto**

A manifesto succeeds when it's a series of **declarative beliefs**, each one specific enough that a reasonable person could disagree. Vague beliefs ("we believe in great customer experiences") aren't manifestos — they're values statements. Real manifestos take positions.

A possible structure for yours:

**Title:** "What we believe about modern commerce" or "The negotiated commerce manifesto"

Then 8-12 declarative beliefs, each 30-80 words. Examples (you should write your own; these are illustrative of the format):

> **The list price is a starting point, not a price.** Every operator knows their customers would buy at different prices. The list price is the most expensive customer's price applied to everyone. Negotiation lets each customer find their own price within your floor.

> **Discounting is a confession that pricing failed.** A 20% storewide isn't a marketing strategy. It's an operator who didn't know what each customer would have paid, so they gave the discount to everyone — including the customers who would have paid full price.

> **CAC is a yield problem, not a media problem.** If your CAC is rising, you don't have a media buying problem. You have a yield problem. Every visitor your store could convert that it didn't is a CAC multiplier.

> **AI agents will be the next 30% of your traffic. They will not accept your list price.** ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot are already shopping for your customers. They negotiate by default. Stores that can't accept agent-submitted offers will lose this traffic to stores that can.

> **The customer who makes an offer is more valuable than the one who clicks "buy."** An offer is a signal — about price sensitivity, willingness to pay, what the customer values. A "buy" click is a data point. Operators who treat offers as signals build buyer portfolios. Operators who treat them as transactions don't.

You can probably write 8-12 of these in a single sitting, drawing from your existing playbooks and OG images. Each one should be something you'd defend if pressed. Each one should make some operators nod and others scoff. If everyone agrees with all of them, the manifesto is too soft.

### **What works for an anti-manifesto**

This is the harder document and the more valuable one. The anti-manifesto's job is to **disqualify the wrong customer**. Done well, it makes the right customer feel seen and the wrong customer self-select out — which protects your support load, your reviews, and your sales conversations.

A possible structure:

**Title:** "Who I Want That\! isn't for" or "If this describes you, don't install our app"

Then a list of specific operator types or beliefs, each one stated bluntly. Examples:

> **You believe your prices are already optimal.** If you're certain your list price captures the right value from every customer, this app won't help you. We're built for operators who know there's elasticity in their market and want to capture it.

> **You think the answer to slow sales is a bigger discount.** Some operators reach for the 40% off button the moment revenue dips. If that's your reflex and you're happy with it, our floors-and-counters approach will feel like overkill. We're built for operators who refuse the storewide sale.

> **You don't carry inventory that needs to move.** Drop-shippers and pure made-to-order brands don't have aged inventory pressure. The economics of CGO are weaker for you. We're built for operators with SKUs in the warehouse and a finite shelf life on them.

> **You measure success in conversion rate alone.** CGO can lower your headline conversion rate while raising your margin and your buyer portfolio value. If conversion rate is your North Star and nothing else matters, you'll dislike what we do.

> **You're not ready to think differently about CAC.** If you're going to evaluate every dollar of our $99/year against an immediate trackable revenue lift, we'll disappoint you. CGO compounds over months. Operators who think in quarters love it. Operators who think in days don't.

The anti-manifesto reads as confident. It also reads as honest. Both qualities are scarce in SaaS marketing and both convert your best buyers.
